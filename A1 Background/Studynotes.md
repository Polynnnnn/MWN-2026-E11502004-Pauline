## 1. Study Notes: Five-Minute Presentation

* **Keep it simple:** Use less text on your slides. People cannot read and listen at the same time. Put only one item on each slide and use very basic designs.
* **Never read from your slides:** Talk directly to your audience.
* **Use beautiful photos:** Pick clear, high-quality pictures that are free to use.
* **Keep data simple:** Show numbers using clear, easy-to-read graphs. Always show the true numbers and give credit to your sources.
* **Repeat your style:** Keep the same look for the whole presentation. Use only 2 or 3 colors and the same text font on every slide.

## 2. Study Notes: How to Read a Paper

**Author:** S. Keshav, University of Waterloo.
**Main Goal:** To give researchers a fast, useful "three-pass" way to read papers and find research without wasting time. 

### The Three-Pass Approach
Do not read a paper from start to finish. Read it in three steps:

*   **The First Pass (The Quick Look):**
    *   This takes about 5 to 10 minutes.
    *   Read the title, abstract, and introduction carefully.
    *   Read only the section titles and skip the rest.
    *   Read the conclusions.
    *   Look quickly at the references to see if you know any of them.
    *   Answer the "Five Cs": Category (what kind of paper is it?), Context (what is it related to?), Correctness (do the ideas make sense?), Contributions (what did they find?), and Clarity (is it well written?).
    *   You can stop reading here if you do not like the paper or if the ideas are bad.

*   **The Second Pass (Understanding the Main Ideas):**
    *   This takes up to one hour.
    *   Read more carefully, but skip hard details like math proofs.
    *   Look closely at pictures and graphs.
    *   Check if graph labels are correct and if the data has error bars.
    *   Mark references you want to read later.
    *   After this, you should be able to tell someone else the main idea of the paper.

*   **The Third Pass (Deep Understanding):**
    *   This takes 1 to 5 hours.
    *   Try to recreate the paper's work in your head using the authors' starting ideas.
    *   Question every idea in every sentence.
    *   Comparing your ideas with the paper helps you find new ideas, hidden mistakes, and missing information.

### Doing a Literature Survey (Finding Related Papers)
This three-step way is great for finding new research:
*   Search for 3 to 5 new papers using keywords.
*   Read them quickly (Pass 1) and look at their related work section to find a summary paper.
*   If there is no summary paper, look at the references to find the most important authors and papers.
*   Go to those authors' websites to see where they publish to find the best conferences.
*   Look at the recent papers from those top conferences.
